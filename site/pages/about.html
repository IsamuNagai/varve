About Varve

Varve is proof-of-work money designed for a century-long horizon: scarce, permissionless, fairly launched, and protected by post-quantum cryptography from its first block. The unit is the varve, the smallest unit is the lamina (1 varve = 100,000,000 laminae), and the ticker is VARV.

The idea

Bitcoin proved that digital money can be scarce without anyone in charge. But the signatures that protect every Bitcoin today could be broken by a large enough quantum computer, and moving a live network to new cryptography is as much a social problem as a technical one. Nobody knows when that day comes. Varve is built so the answer never depends on it: quantum-resistant signatures from genesis, the ability to change cryptography by plan rather than by crisis, and a launch where no one, including its creator, starts ahead.

Design principles

  • Correctness
  • Security
  • Simplicity
  • Cryptographic adaptability
  • Independent verification
  • Predictable issuance
  • Permissionless participation

What makes it different

  • Post-quantum signatures from genesis: ML-DSA (FIPS 204) for everyday spending and SLH-DSA (FIPS 205), which relies only on hash functions, as an independent second family.
  • Cryptographic agility: typed outputs and a defined lifecycle, so schemes can be introduced and retired in an orderly way, without a restart.
  • Built-in recovery: native timelocked recovery paths for inheritance and disaster recovery.
  • CPU-friendly mining: RandomX v2 with a Varve-specific salt.
  • Sound, predictable issuance: a smooth 8-year half-life and a small permanent tail that funds security forever.
  • A truly fair launch: genesis derived from six future Bitcoin blocks; no premine and no allocation.

What Varve deliberately does not build

  • A smart-contract virtual machine. Every feature is permanent attack surface.
  • A token platform or a place to issue other assets.
  • A presale, ICO, premine, founder or team allocation, or airdrop. Ever.

Who builds it

Varve was started by Isamu Nagai, who writes the specification, the reference node and the development journal. Follow updates on X. By design, nothing in Varve depends on any one person: the specification, code and every decision are public, so anyone can verify, rebuild and continue the work.
